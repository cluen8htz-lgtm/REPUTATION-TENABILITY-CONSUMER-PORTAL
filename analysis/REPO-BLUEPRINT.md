# Repossession Blueprint (Toyota Motor Credit pattern — module 2 reference)

## Core theory
The retail installment contract's receivable was securitized: the named
creditor (e.g., Toyota Motor Credit) sold the paper into a trust (Toyota
Auto Receivables — TMVT/TMNT). From that sale date, the named creditor
retained NO security interest, yet its notices and the repossession order
presented it as creditor/secured party. The DMV title still shows the old
lienholder (bonded-title issue). Result: repossession by an entity with no
present right to possession — 15 U.S.C. §1692f(6); UCC 9-609.

## Statutory frame
- UCC Article 9 Part 6: 9-609 (self-help, no breach of peace), 9-610
  (commercially reasonable), 9-611 (notification), 9-613 (contents/form —
  must identify the secured party; differs when receivables are sold),
  9-615 (proceeds/deficiency), 9-616 (deficiency explanation), 9-620–622
  (strict foreclosure), 9-623 (redemption).
- Florida: Fla. Stat. §679.609 self-help; §§679.610–616, 679.623 companions.
- California: Com. Code Div. 9 equivalents.
- FDCPA: §1692a(6) (repo agency = debt collector), §1692d/e, §1692f(6)
  (dispossession without present right to possession).

## The eight violation patterns (REPO-001…008)
1. No present right to possession (paper sold to trust; §1692f(6)).
2. §9-613 notice fails to identify true secured party (trust not disclosed).
3. No time/place/date of sale → redemption rights (§9-623) destroyed.
4. Disposition not commercially reasonable (9-610/615).
5. Breach of the peace (9-609(2)(b)).
6. Repo agency FDCPA liability (§1692a(6), 1692d/e/f).
7. Deficiency without §9-616 explanation / inflated from bad sale.
8. Securitization concealment — misrepresentation of character & capacity.

UCC-1 of record → Sale & Servicing Agreement (424B5 exhibit or tender-offer
copies — retrieval cost reported) → RC e-sign timestamp vs. receivable sale
cutoff.

## Case anchors (upload full PACER files first)
- Wright v. Santander Consumer USA, Inc. — M.D. Fla. — federal-complaint
  structure, wrongful repossession theory.
- Leal v. Toyota Motor Credit Corp. — California — Toyota/trust authority,
  embodies the SEC-attorney + consumer-attorney frame.

## Deliverables per case
1. Document demand (certified mail, 30 days) — full §9-611/613/616 set,
   RC, UCC-1, authority docs, SSAG, repo work order.
2. SEC due diligence report (timeline + secured-party-of-record audit).
3. Violation report (REPO-001…008, FL/CA + federal).
4. Notice of demand.
5. Federal complaint (Wright structure; caption placeholders).
6. SPECIAL REPORT — how the creditor chose securitization (no retained
   interest) yet repossessed as secured party; material misrepresentation;
   evidence table mapping each exhibit to each conclusion.
